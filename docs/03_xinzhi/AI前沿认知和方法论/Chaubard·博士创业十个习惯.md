## 一句话

同一堂课的第 31 篇讲「两个陷阱」，这一页回到一手：**研究者的本能不是创业的短板，恰恰是他最引以为傲的那部分**——所以「戒掉」比「学会」更疼。

开场定调：

> 🔵 “Some of the most successful companies in the world were created by founders with PhDs, including Google, Anthropic, Databricks, Together AI, and more. But these are exceptions to the rule… many of the skills you're rewarded for as a researcher can be antithetical, or at least orthogonal, to succeed as a founder.”

> 🔵 “To become a formidable founder, you will literally have to kill part of your academic identity.”

---

## 一、别优化虚荣指标：分数、引用量、演讲场次

第一条直指评价体系。他引了 Paul Graham 的原话：

> 🔵 “The most damaging thing you learned in school wasn't something you learn in any specific class. It was learning to get good grades.”

创始人思维问的是另一组问题——别人**真会为它付钱吗**，还是只是「说会」：

> 🔵 “The founder mindset is entirely different. It asks, what is something people actually want? What will they actually pay money for versus what they say they would pay money for?”

---

## 二、有趣的问题 ≠ 重要的问题

科研奖励「深挖机理」，但深挖常对任何人的日常生活没有影响；创始人要能说出那句让学者难受的话：

> 🔵 “In founder mode, you have to be okay with saying, it just works. We don't know exactly why, but that's good enough. Ship it.”

他借 Marc Andreessen 收口：“The only thing that matters is getting to product market fit.”

---

## 三、抄袭是正当策略：MIT 式与斯坦福式

学术世界里 novelty 是生死线，创业世界里反过来。「MIT 式／斯坦福式」这组区分他明确归给 **Joe Lonsdale**：

> 🔵 “Joe Lonsdale makes a brilliant distinction between MIT style startups and Stanford style startups. MIT startups often revolve around a brand new invention, a brilliant black box that we patented… no one really wants your black box. A Stanford-style startup, on the other hand, starts with a single boring customer problem and iterates on it forever.”

证据给得密集：Instacart 做了 15 年还在迭代同一件事、且**没有发明**线上买菜；Lyft 对 Uber、Anthropic 对 OpenAI、Rappi 对 Instacart——抄打法不丢人，解真问题就行。再引乔布斯收尾：“You have to start with the customer experience and walk backwards to the technology.”

---

## 四、没有交卷日：把花生酱涂厚，别涂薄

科研总有「投出去那一刻」，创业没有终点。研究者的广撒网习惯（五个方向同时开）是学术上的广度优先，在创始状态里是**把花生酱涂得太薄**：

> 🔵 “You have to wake up every single day and crank on that exact problem without getting sidetracked. For decades. Many academics just get bored, and this distracts their team from the core mission.”

---

## 五、上飞机，去见用户——投资人不算数

> 🔵 “When I say talk to people, I do not mean investors. Most investors have no clue what is going to be a great product or not.”

然后是他自己的学费——Focal 花三个月训练零售店内人流追踪模型：

> 🔵 “Every retailer told us this was a major problem. Three months of work later… it told them that people spend a lot of time in the checkout counter and the deli counter at lunchtime. I asked them, will you pay for this? And they said, absolutely not, because there was no value delivered. I could have manually labeled a few videos a day and given them the same data and gotten the same conclusion.”

⚪ 这段是全篇最硬的方法论，也是给 AI 创业公司的具体指令：**先人肉闭环，再花算力**——

> 🔵 “If you are an AI company, start with a human in the loop. Validate that your customers actually want what you're serving before you spend millions on compute training a model.”

---

## 六、销售话术允许不严谨

> 🔵 “15 minutes could save you 15% or more on your car insurance. What a beautifully inexact statement… If you obsess over rigor in your marketing, it will destroy you. By no means should you lie, but using punchier language is necessary to get people excited.”

---

## 七、70% 原则：速度赢过完美信息

引贝索斯：多数决策在拿到**期望信息的七成**时就该做。学术上七成把握发不了 NeurIPS，创业里等 100% 就太晚了。他给的历史脚注是数据库战争：

> 🔵 “In the great database wars, Michael Stonebraker had a technically superior product, QUEL, but Larry Ellison, founder of Oracle, pushed SQL into the market faster… Ellison famously said, QUEL might be better than SQL, I don't care. SQL is going to win. And he was right, speed beats perfect information.”

---

## 八、脏活不能外包

> 🔵 “A lot of academics love the idea of a startup, but they don't actually want to do these messy jobs. They think, oh, I'll just hire an MBA to do the business stuff. And I will do the cool research on VC's dime. This is a terrible idea.”

理由不是勤勉，是认知：**离开销售就长不出客户同理心，没有同理心就做不出客户真要的产品**。

---

## 九、换一副更进取的心态

> 🔵 “You must become a capitalist. Starting a company is the most capitalistic thing you can possibly do, and frankly, a lot of academics are deeply uncomfortable with that. You have to be comfortable charging people money for things, winning talent, making 80% margins, raising capital, and most importantly, beating your competition.”

他转述 Larry Ellison 式的世界观：对手想干掉你的公司、抢走你孩子嘴里的饭——这就是你要进的场子。

---

## 十、没人关心你有多聪明

收尾一条最疼。学者会主动讲自动机、讲 P 与 NP 的看法，但这在销售、产品、融资里全都失效：

> 🔵 “You need an innate curiosity about people… you only get that perspective by listening… As Dale Carnegie once wrote, you can make more friends in two months by becoming interested in other people than you can in two years by trying to get others interested in you.”

---

## 收尾：一张书单和一句甘地

> 🔵 “Get out of your office, get on a flight, and go meet with your users where they live and work. Validate your assumptions. Will people actually pay you for this? Because that is exactly what your competition is doing.”

> 🔵 “If you want to quiet your academic brain and start growing your founder brain, I highly recommend reading SPIN Selling, High Output Management, The Seven Habits of Highly Effective People, and How to Win Friends and Influence People.”

> 🔵 “If you want to change the world, start with yourself. You already have the intelligence. The only question left is, do you have the will?”
